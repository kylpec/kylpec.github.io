Overview

Sunbelt Rentals needed to modernize the digital infrastructure supporting its nationwide equipment rental operation, including Gearbox, an internal application used by more than 10,000 employees across 950 locations.

As a UX/UI Designer, I helped improve the system around an increasingly complex rental process shaped by store-based pickup and returns, equipment delivery delays, and the specialized needs of large, remote distribution centers. The work included updating the interface, developing mobile capabilities for employees in the field, and rethinking physical checkout workflows through more accurate item tracking. Together, these improvements created a more connected digital ecosystem designed to increase efficiency, convenience, and operational reliability.

My Role

I led the design-system work and visual direction for the new experience. That included auditing the legacy interface, defining the component architecture, and establishing a Gearbox-specific library within Sunbelt’s global system.

I directed two designers through the library buildout and carried the system into core product workflows, while also shaping the product identity, illustration language, and motion communication.

Legacy Audit

With no reliable component inventory or original design team to reference, the existing interface became the source of truth. I cataloged recurring patterns, traced inconsistent behavior, and separated essential functionality from accumulated visual debt.

The audit established what needed to be preserved, consolidated, or rebuilt before the new system could take shape.

Component Library

Gearbox required specialized components, but not an isolated design language. I structured the library as a product-specific layer within Sunbelt’s global system, aligning shared foundations while accounting for the density and operational demands of the platform.

I established the architecture and documentation, then directed two designers as the library expanded and evolved.

Product UI

Applying the system across asset management, dashboards, reporting, permissions, site operations, and dense data tables exposed the conditions the library had to withstand.

Edge cases discovered within the product fed back into the components, keeping the system grounded in actual workflows rather than idealized examples.

Product Identity

I developed the initial logo for the application. The cube-like form and modular cutouts suggest building blocks coming together, while the 45-degree angles reference the existing Sunbelt Rentals identity.

Spot Illustration

A focused illustration language for system states and moments of feedback.

Motion Explainer

Because Gearbox combines a physical rental-yard process with a digital workflow, I created an animated site map to clearly explain the user journey and corresponding system activity.
